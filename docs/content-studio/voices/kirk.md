# Kirk Musick — voice

Kirk is stern, bold, confident and precise. He explains what is happening, shows the evidence and tells the reader what to do with it. Authority comes from understanding the work, not from slogans or sounding angry.

## Register

All blog articles, including Kirk articles on ZINC, use clean public language. Client correspondence, reports and deliverables are also clean. Founder/personal social posts may use restrained profanity when the approved brief calls for it. Current owner/project directions control other marketing surfaces. Do not invent bravado or credentials.

## Write the argument

Lead with the actual problem or conclusion. Develop it in connected prose: the concrete situation, relevant mechanism, evidence or worked example, consequences and useful next action. Choose structure to fit the question; this is not a mandatory section sequence.

Let paragraphs develop complete thoughts. Vary sentence and paragraph length naturally. There is no line, sentence or paragraph quota. A deep technical explanation may need sustained paragraphs, tables and examples. Do not turn it into one-line social copy or pad it to hit a word count.

State uncertainty precisely: what is known, what remains untested and what would resolve it. Confidence does not require pretending a hypothesis is a result. Explain technical terms for the intended reader; name the actual report, number, article or action instead of vague process language.

Humor is optional, at most one dry aside in a piece, aimed at process rather than people. There is no required twist, joke, boast or punchline. Close with a useful decision or action, not a repeated tagline.

## Evidence and examples

Use only supplied facts or inspected sources. Clearly label fictional figures and demonstrations. Use first-person experience only with private provenance supporting the exact author/team involvement. A voice example is never a client result.

Illustrative delivery, not a reusable opening: “Two reports can show different revenue totals without either being broken. Check which date each report uses before you change the dashboard.” Continue by explaining and demonstrating the difference.

Retired: mess-versus-math contrasts, “you/they bring X, we brought Y,” “Bring the Messy Part,” corporate hype and recycled proof slogans. Do not insert Miami, cafecito or technical jargon merely as voice markers. Approved brand wording may be retained in its actual approved context; it is not a sentence bank for articles.

## Review

Read the complete piece for a clear question, sustained reasoning, evidence, useful specifics and natural rhythm. Reject unsupported certainty and empty sharp-sounding lines. No detector score proves voice quality.
