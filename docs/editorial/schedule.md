## Editorial standard

Every article must resolve a reader's specific question. How-tos include prerequisites, steps, an actual worked example or usable template, and a way to tell whether the setup worked. Label example data; never invent client results. Analysis separates what changed, what the evidence supports, and our interpretation. Open with the actual issue, vary structure to suit it, remove generic scene-setting and padded summaries. Personality comes from judgment and specificity, not an artificial accent or joke quota.

Owner requirement, 3 November 2026 CT: reader value is critical across the entire blog library. Every article must answer its main question, explain the reasoning, show a concrete example and leave the intended reader with a useful action and a way to check it. Review the complete manuscript against its title and reader task; a word count, table, image, download or detector score alone cannot establish that it delivers value. Kirk is stern, bold and confident in Kirk Voice; Jaymie is light and playful; Wendy is warm and conversational, with British quirk and wit grounded in the owner's writing references. Use available content-writing tools and real editorial revision; remove internal notes, prompts and unsupported claims, without promising detector immunity.

Every blog needs a full, deduplicated list of its actual supporting sources at the bottom, useful semantic tables, accurate structured data that agrees with visible content, a topic-specific explanatory illustration and a usable reader download with instructions. Test the file, its contents and its public route. Keep specimen data labelled and untested results pending. Use service-aligned blog categories as the pillars, house distinct guides and supporting articles within them, and connect relevant articles and services where they answer the reader's next question. Check existing coverage before commissioning another URL; avoid repeated intent, forced links and decorative resources.

Owner requirement, 3 November 2026 CT: use our real client work as anonymised personal or team experience wherever it helps the reader. Consult the relevant client-work inventory, retain private provenance for the exact claim, and explain what we did, the decision and the useful lesson. Remove names, domains, logos, distinctive identifying details and confidential material. Use “I” only when the bylined author's involvement is established; otherwise use “we” or “our team”. Do not invent conversations, difficulties, outcomes or measurements, turn fictional examples into experience, or describe ongoing work as delivered. This does not change client-removal requirements or authorise access to private sources.

Use fresh primary sources for security/platform claims. Recheck time-sensitive claims at publication; distinguish affected software/configurations and supported versions. No claims that WordPress is universally unsafe, Astro is unhackable, schema guarantees rankings, or AI tools guarantee inclusion.

Write for business owners, not fellow developers. Explain necessary technical terms plainly. Astro itself does not use PHP; retaining WordPress for editing retains PHP on that side.

Use current approved typography/layout. Match the existing light studio illustration family while varying composition by topic: black equipment, restrained teal accents, white mugs and a matte black code() notebook where useful. Each released article gets a purposeful featured image, descriptive alt text, excerpt, unique metadata, actual author identity, Article schema, useful existing internal links and real dates. Prefer diagrams, annotated examples or contextual artwork over stock robots and generic glowing graphics. Test relevant desktop/mobile rendering and tables. Do not alter shared designs as part of an article.
