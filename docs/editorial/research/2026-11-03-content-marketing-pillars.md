## Reviewed methods

| Source | Useful method | Application to ZINC |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [HubSpot topic documentation](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/content-strategy/pillar-pages-topics-and-subtopics) | Group customer questions and supporting content; inspect links. The tool's grouping alone does not directly change SEO. | Keep existing service categories as the pillars. A guide remains an article within its category. |
| [Semrush topic clusters](https://www.semrush.com/blog/topic-clusters/) | Start with business and audience needs, map distinct intents, connect relevant pages and review the cluster. | Inspect the current inventory before commissioning; give each article a distinct question and relevant next page. |
| [Ahrefs content hubs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/content-hub/) | Connect a hub with useful supporting pages and provide routes in both directions. | Preserve article/category links and add the missing service-to-category route. Link peer articles where the reader needs the next explanation. |
| [Animalz information gain](https://www.animalz.co/blog/information-gain) | Make an additive contribution through specific insight, evidence and useful application. | Use verified, anonymised experience and concrete examples rather than another general summary. Do not treat its patent discussion as a confirmed ranking mechanism. |
| [Google helpful-content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) | Assess whether the intended audience can accomplish its goal; do not write to arbitrary word counts. | Review the complete article against its promise. Structural completeness supports that review but does not prove quality. |

These are editorial methods, not purchased tools or promises of rankings, AI citations or leads. HubSpot and Ahrefs use a guide-as-pillar convention; that terminology does not override the owner's category-as-pillar model.
